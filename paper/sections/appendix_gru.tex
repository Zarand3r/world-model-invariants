\section{The GRU precursor study}
\label{sec:gru}

The method in this paper was developed on a much smaller recurrent model before it met a pixel
world model. That study is unpublished and has no separate reference, so we describe it here in
enough detail to be judged. Its role is not to support the DreamerV3 result. It is where the
phenomenon first appeared, where several appealing readings of it were killed, and where the
narrower claim tested in the main text came from.

\paragraph{Setup.} A single-layer GRU of 48--64 hidden units, about 13k parameters, trained on
next-observation prediction for low-dimensional mechanical systems. The model saw \emph{position
only}, so any representation of velocity had to be reconstructed internally by the recurrent state.
We studied two conservative systems, an anharmonic central-force system and a pair of coupled
anharmonic oscillators, with matched dissipative variants trained as controls. Extraction never used
a physical label; ground truth entered only when scoring a recovered quantity.

\paragraph{The search.} Writing $z$ for a low-dimensional representation of the hidden state and $f$
for the latent flow induced by the trained transition, we look jointly for a scalar $C$ and an
antisymmetric field $B$ with $f \approx B\nabla C$. Antisymmetry gives $\nabla C^\top B \nabla C = 0$,
so any $C$ satisfying the fit is approximately constant along the flow. We score the fit by the
normalised residual $R = \|f - B\nabla C\|^2 / \|f\|^2$.

\subsection{What it established}

\paragraph{Conservation alone is not evidence.} A randomly initialised GRU already contained a latent
scalar correlating with true energy at $|\rho| \approx 0.998$. A high-dimensional random encoding of
a low-dimensional trajectory retains enough information for a flexible readout to reconstruct energy.
This is the result that forced every later claim to be a \emph{difference} against a null rather than
a level, and it is why the main text leads with the same control on DreamerV3.

\paragraph{Flow-based selection separated conservative from dissipative models.} Once candidates were
selected by the joint criterion, the recovered scalar correlated with true energy at
$|\rho_E| \ge 0.972$ on conservative models, often approaching $0.999$, and the residual separated the
two arms strongly ($p = 5.4\times10^{-6}$, $d = 3.10$ on a representative comparison).

\paragraph{Enforcing the recovered quantity improved rollouts.} Correcting the hidden state during
free rollout so that $C$ stayed near its initial value reduced trajectory error by up to $68\%$ and
true-energy drift by $69\%$, and \emph{harmed} dissipative models. This is the first version of the
recover, refuse, intervene chain that the main text retests.

\paragraph{Long-horizon error is mostly phase.} About $77\%$ of rollout error lay along the true
physical flow and only $14\%$ changed the conserved quantity. The model stayed near the right orbit
and moved along it at the wrong rate.

\paragraph{The frequency--action mechanism.} In a nonlinear oscillator the frequency depends on the
conserved quantity, so a small energy error produces a persistent frequency error that accumulates as
phase. With $\kappa(E) = d\ln\omega/dE$ and $X(t) = \int_0^t \kappa(E_s)\,\Delta E(s)\,ds$, this
predictor beat generic functions of time on held-out data.

The coefficient sits near the natural scale the mechanism predicts, and it was never fitted for that
purpose: $\kappa$ came from the simulator. Adding an oscillatory correction raises the central-force
fit to $0.703$, while adding an angular-momentum term moves it to $0.657$, slightly worse, and the
coupled system has no rotational symmetry at all. The scalar-energy account is therefore already
complete for the errors these models actually make. A one-time intervention on the invariant, after
which the model ran free, changed later phase drift with the predicted sign in $94\%$ of cases and a
held-out $R^2$ of $0.887$.

\subsection{What it killed, and why that shapes the main text}

\paragraph{$\nabla C$ is not the physical energy gradient.} Matching perturbation directions so each
produced the same instantaneous change in true energy did \emph{not} equalise their downstream
effects. Displacing along $\nabla C$ moved later phase drift most per unit of energy change, a random
direction less, and a direction tangent to $C$ least, on every seed. The size of the gap depends on
how the coefficient is estimated, so we report the ordering rather than the digits; what matters is
that instantaneous $\Delta E$ is not a sufficient mediator. Separately, $\nabla C$ moved true energy
only $1.08\times$ more per unit displacement than a random direction. A model can hold the right
scalar while its surrounding latent geometry is wrong.

\paragraph{A symmetry the model gets wrong.} For a rotationally symmetric central force the radial
frequency must be even in angular momentum, $\omega_r(E, L) = \omega_r(E, -L)$. Measured at fixed
energy, the simulator obeys this: $|L|$ alone predicts its radial frequency at $R^2 = 0.89$, and
signed $L$ adds nothing ($R^2 = 0.00$). The learned GRU does not. Signed $L$ predicts its frequency
at $R^2 = 0.30$, a dependence the physics forbids exactly, and it is the larger of the two effects
the model shows. Recovering an energy-like scalar is therefore not the same as recovering the law.

\paragraph{Structural readings that failed.} A low-dimensional part of the hidden state carried most
of the information reaching decoded futures, which suggested a quotient interpretation; the
corresponding divergence statistic then separated conservative from dissipative systems at only
$\mathrm{AUC} = 0.68$, so we keep the observation as description rather than explanation. A subspace
chosen by transition-closure criteria did not beat plain PCA at matched dimension. Extraction was
also sensitive to representation: whitening the latent could destroy recovery, and keeping only the
highest-variance directions removed physical information, which is the result that reproduces most
strongly in the main text.

\subsection{What transferred}

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
finding & GRU & DreamerV3 (this paper) \\
\midrule
energy-like invariant recoverable & $|\rho_E| \ge 0.972$ & replicates, $\approx 0.97$ \\
untrained nets score high & up to $0.998$ & replicates, up to $0.908$ \\
conservative vs dissipative separate & $p = 5.4\times10^{-6}$ & replicates, no seed overlap \\
enforcing it improves rollouts & up to $68\%$ & direction only, $3.3\%$ \\
residual predicts how physical $C$ is & works & \textbf{does not transfer} \\
frequency weighting earns its place & $R^2\ 0.658$ vs $0.131$ & \textbf{unresolved} \\
$\nabla C$ is the physical direction & \textbf{false} & remains unsupported \\
right scalar implies right geometry & \textbf{false} & motivates the chart reading \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two rows carry most of the weight. The intervention transfers in direction and specificity but not in
magnitude, and Section~\ref{sec:intervention} explains why the two percentages are not comparable.
The flow-generation residual, which was the study's most distinctive instrument, does not transfer at
all: on DreamerV3 it is mid-pack against random polynomials, and conservation does the work instead.
The value of the precursor for this paper is that the DreamerV3 result did not come from one
successful experiment. It came from a programme that first produced the phenomenon and then removed
several tempting readings of it before the surviving claim was put to a standard pixel world model.
