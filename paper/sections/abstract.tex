\begin{abstract}
World models can predict what happens next without necessarily learning the physics behind it. We
ask a stronger question than whether physical quantities can be decoded from a model's latent state:
does the model learn something its own dynamics actually preserve? In DreamerV3 trained only on
pendulum video, we find a reproducible energy-like invariant without using physical labels, while
the same search fails on matched damped systems where no such invariant should exist. The invariant
begins to drift when the model imagines forward on its own, and correcting that drift improves its
predictions; random corrections usually make them worse. This suggests that world models can learn
real physical structure but still fail to respect it during rollout, and that preserving learned
invariants may be a useful way to make their predictions more reliable.
\end{abstract}
