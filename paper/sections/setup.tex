\section{Setup and method}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model and data.} We analyze the published DreamerV3 architecture
\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3}: 13.5M parameters, categorical $32\times32$ stochastic latents, a
convolutional encoder and decoder, Kullback--Leibler balancing, and unimix. We train only the world
model, with no actor or critic.

The data come from Gymnasium's \texttt{Pendulum-v1} \citep{towers2024gymnasium}. Each simulator step
renders a $500\times500$ frame. We crop it to $448\times448$ and block-average to $64\times64$. We
set the simulator state directly, sampling $\theta\sim U[-1.8,1.8]$ and
$\dot\theta\sim U[-2.2,2.2]$. We reject any trajectory that reaches the simulator's $|\dot\theta|=8$
speed clip because clipping would break conservation. Actions are always zero. A single frame does
not reveal velocity, so the model must infer $\dot\theta$ from the sequence.

We train on a fixed dataset of 256 trajectories, each 120 frames long, using Adam at $10^{-4}$,
batch size 16, and sequence length 64. We cap each run at 30 minutes of wall-clock time, which gives
about 6{,}500 gradient steps. Three independently trained seeds all pass checks fixed before
analysis: KL divergence above 1 nat, one-step decoding at least $4\times$ better than predicting the
dataset mean, and finite rollouts. Appendix~\ref{sec:repro} gives the remaining training details.
Our claims concern a DreamerV3 world model trained on pendulum video, not DreamerV3 as a full
reinforcement-learning agent.

\paragraph{Three kinds of latent trajectories.} We use three different latent trajectories and keep
them separate throughout the paper.

An \emph{observation-conditioned} trajectory is the sequence of deterministic recurrent states
produced when the model receives the next real frame at every step. This is the latent sequence
returned by the encoder on held-out video.

The \emph{one-step transition} $T$ is the model's autonomous recurrent update with zero action and
no new observation. It maps the current recurrent state to the next one.

An \emph{imagination rollout} starts from one encoded state and repeatedly applies $T$ without
supplying any later frames.

To estimate the latent flow, we apply $T$ once at each state from an observation-conditioned
trajectory. The transition is therefore autonomous even though the states at which we evaluate it
come from real video. Sections~\ref{sec:recovery} and~\ref{sec:refusal} measure conservation along
observation-conditioned trajectories. Section~\ref{sec:intervention} then tests what happens when
the same scalar drifts during autonomous imagination.

\paragraph{Latent coordinates.} After training, we freeze the model. Let $h$ denote the
deterministic recurrent state on held-out video and $\bar h$ its mean. We take the top 12 principal
directions of $h$, remove any direction with no support in the data, and let $P$ denote the
resulting orthonormal map. All extraction, projection, and perturbation operate on
\begin{equation}
z = P^\top(h-\bar h).
\label{eq:z}
\end{equation}
Let $F(z)$ be the projection through $P$ of the model's one-step displacement $T(h)-h$. We use the
model's own transition displacement directly rather than fitting a separate vector field and
analyzing that surrogate.

\paragraph{The candidate family.} We look for the invariant among degree-4 polynomials in $z$.
Writing $\varphi(z)$ for the vector of monomials up to degree 4 in the 12 latent coordinates, a
candidate is $C(z) = a^\top\varphi(z)$, and the search is over the coefficient vector $a$. There are
1819 such monomials, from $z_1$ through $z_1z_2z_3z_4$; we exclude the constant term, which is
conserved trivially. Degree 4 is a compromise. The pendulum's energy is quadratic in $\dot\theta$
and needs polynomial terms to approximate $\cos\theta$, and the latent is some unknown nonlinear
encoding of $(\theta, \dot\theta)$ rather than those coordinates themselves, so the family has to be
rich enough to express energy through that distortion while staying small enough to fit.

\paragraph{The invariance objective.} An invariant is constant along any one trajectory and differs
between trajectories. We therefore score a candidate by
\begin{equation}
\text{ratio}(C) \;=\;
\frac{\text{mean within-trajectory variance of } C}{\text{total variance of } C},
\label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
which is $0$ for a perfectly conserved scalar and near $1$ for one that wanders as much within a
trajectory as across the dataset. The denominator is what makes the objective non-trivial: without
it, $C = 0$ would score perfectly while distinguishing nothing.

Both variances are quadratic forms in $a$. With $W$ the mean within-trajectory covariance of the
features and $T$ their total covariance, Equation~\ref{eq:ratio} is $a^\top W a / a^\top T a$, a
Rayleigh quotient. Its minimiser is therefore not something we search for iteratively: it is the
eigenvector of the generalized problem $W a = \lambda\, T a$ with the smallest eigenvalue, and
$\lambda$ is the invariance ratio itself. One eigendecomposition returns the whole family, ranked
from most to least conserved.

\paragraph{Requiring the scalar to generate the flow.} Conservation alone does not pick out a unique
scalar. If $C$ is conserved then so is any function of it, so $E$, $E^2$ and mixtures of $E$ with
other conserved quantities all sit near the top of the ranking, and the leading eigenvector is
generally some combination of them rather than energy. We therefore ask $C$ to do more than stay
constant: we ask it to generate the motion. We fit $C$ jointly with an antisymmetric operator $B$
such that
\begin{equation}
F \approx B\nabla C, \qquad B^\top = -B.
\label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
This is Hamiltonian mechanics written without coordinates. In $\dot q = \partial H/\partial p$,
$\dot p = -\partial H/\partial q$, the state moves orthogonally to the energy gradient, and
multiplication by an antisymmetric matrix is what rotating a gradient that way looks like in matrix
form. The relevant identity is that $\nabla C^\top B \nabla C = 0$ for any antisymmetric $B$, so if
$F = B\nabla C$ then $C$ is constant along the flow by construction rather than by fitting. The
condition also separates candidates that the invariance ratio cannot: $E^2$ is exactly as conserved
as $E$ but does not pair with the same $B$, so the requirement selects a direction inside the
conserved family \citep{arnold1989mathematical}.

The fit is bilinear, so fixing either $a$ or $B$ makes the other a least-squares problem and we
alternate. It searches within the top eight eigenvectors from Equation~\ref{eq:ratio}, so the
recovered $C$ has eight effective degrees of freedom rather than 1819. That matters for a search run
on 52 trajectories: a free fit over the full basis could drive the in-sample ratio to zero by
overfitting. Section~\ref{sec:ablations} tests what the second condition contributes, and the answer
is less than its prominence here suggests.

\paragraph{What ``no physical labels'' means.} The search sees only latent states and the model's own
one-step transition. It never sees $\theta$, $\dot\theta$ or the energy $E$, and nothing in
Equations~\ref{eq:ratio} and~\ref{eq:pair} refers to them. Ground truth enters afterward, when we
score the recovered $C$ against $E$ to report $|\rho|_E$. One hyperparameter is an exception worth
stating: the extraction dimension was chosen with reference to energy correlation, on a sweep over
three models that are not among the three we report. It was then fixed and applied unchanged here,
which is why Section~\ref{sec:recovery} describes it as fixed before these models were evaluated.

\paragraph{Reference energy.} We compare $C$ with the textbook pendulum energy
\begin{equation*}
E = \tfrac{1}{6}\dot\theta^2 + 5\cos\theta.
\end{equation*}
Gymnasium uses a semi-implicit integrator, so it conserves a nearby shadow Hamiltonian
$\tilde H = H + O(\Delta t)$ rather than $E$ itself \citep{hairer2006geometric}. In our trajectories,
this discretization produces about 12\% relative oscillation in $E$ without secular drift. We treat
that mismatch as a noise floor.

\paragraph{Earlier recurrent-model study.} We developed the extraction method on a smaller recurrent
model before applying it to DreamerV3. That work is unpublished, so it does not count as evidence
for the DreamerV3 results. Appendix~\ref{sec:gru} summarizes it only to document the origin of the
method and the controls that we retest here.
