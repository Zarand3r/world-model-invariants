\section{The model contains a conserved quantity, and the level alone does not show it}
\label{sec:recovery}

At the extraction dimension we registered in advance, the recovered $C$ correlates with true energy
at $|\rho|_E = 0.973$, $0.967$, and $0.975$ across three seeds the choice never saw. The recovered
quantity stays constant along the trajectories the model encodes from held-out video: its
within-trajectory variance is $1.1\times10^{-4}$ of its total variance, so it is nearly flat along a
trajectory and varies between trajectories, which is what a conserved quantity does. Two cautions
about that number. We measure it where an observation corrects each step; under the model's
own imagination the same $C$ drifts far more, which is what Section~\ref{sec:intervention} exploits.
And it is a different statistic from the \emph{held-out invariance ratio} of
Table~\ref{tab:refusal}, which scores the invariance-only candidate rather than the jointly fitted
$C$; the two are not comparable and we report both.

That number on its own is close to meaningless, and the reason is the same one that makes probing
weak evidence \citep{hewitt2019control,belinkov2022probing}. Our search returns functions that stay
constant within a trajectory and vary between trajectories. On a one-degree-of-freedom conservative
pendulum, energy is essentially the only such function, so any faithful encoding of $(\theta,
\dot\theta)$ will yield something correlated with it. A randomly initialised network encodes the
frame history perfectly well.

\paragraph{An untrained model clears the gate we registered.} We ran the identical pipeline on six
randomly initialised DreamerV3 models. They score $|\rho|_E$ from $0.170$ to $0.908$, and four of
the six clear the $0.7$ threshold we had registered as the success criterion
(Figure~\ref{fig:three}a). The threshold measured nothing about learning. We had carried it over
from an earlier architecture without checking it against this one's null.

The honest comparison is a difference, and it separates cleanly on two statistics at once. Every
trained seed scores above every untrained draw ($0.967$ against $0.908$), and every trained seed has
a lower pairing residual than every untrained draw ($0.865$ against $0.912$). Neither range overlaps.

\paragraph{Consistency separates trained from untrained more sharply than level does.} The three
trained seeds span $0.008$ in $|\rho|_E$. The six untrained draws span $0.738$, which is $92\times$
wider. A random network sometimes lands near $0.9$ and sometimes at $0.17$, depending on its
initialisation. The trained models land at $0.97$ every time.

\paragraph{Takeaway.} Training does not create energy information in the latent, since an untrained
network often has it. Training makes the recovered structure reproducible across seeds and conserved
under the model's own transition. That is the claim the evidence supports, and it is narrower than
the one the raw correlation suggests.
