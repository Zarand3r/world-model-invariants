\section{Training makes invariant recovery reproducible}
\label{sec:recovery}

At the extraction dimension fixed in advance, the three trained conservative models recover scalars
with $|\rho|_E = 0.973$, $0.967$, and $0.975$. Along observation-conditioned trajectories, the
recovered $C$ has within-trajectory variance equal to $1.1\times10^{-4}$ of its total variance. It is
therefore nearly constant within a trajectory while still varying across trajectories.
Table~\ref{tab:refusal} reports this quantity as the drift of $C$. The held-out invariance ratio in
the next row evaluates the invariance-only candidate rather than the jointly fitted $C$, so the two
statistics should not be compared as if they measured the same object.

The energy correlation alone is weak evidence \citep{hewitt2019control,belinkov2022probing}. Our
search favors functions that remain constant within a trajectory and differ across trajectories. For
a one-degree-of-freedom conservative pendulum, energy is essentially the only nontrivial scalar with
that property. A latent representation that preserves enough information about
$(\theta,\dot\theta)$ can therefore produce a strong energy-correlated readout even if the model
never learned to use energy dynamically.

The untrained baseline confirms this concern. Applying the identical pipeline to six randomly
initialized DreamerV3 models gives $|\rho|_E$ values from $0.170$ to $0.908$. Four of the six exceed
the $0.7$ success threshold that we had inherited from the earlier architecture. That threshold
therefore does not identify learning in DreamerV3.

The trained and untrained groups still differ in two ways. Every trained model scores above every
untrained draw in energy correlation ($0.967$ versus $0.908$ at the closest pair), and every trained
model has a lower pairing residual than every untrained draw ($0.865$ versus $0.912$). With only
three trained seeds and six random draws, these non-overlaps are suggestive rather than
statistically decisive, so we do not attach a $p$-value to them.

The more robust difference is reproducibility. The three trained models cluster tightly between
$0.967$ and $0.975$, whereas the six untrained models span $0.170$--$0.908$. Training therefore
appears to make the recovered scalar reproducible across independently trained models rather than
merely making energy decodable.
