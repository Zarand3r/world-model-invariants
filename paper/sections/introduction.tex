\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A world model that predicts video well might have learned physics, or it might have learned to
interpolate video. Telling those apart matters if we want to plan with such a model, trust it out of
distribution, or design a better one. The usual evidence is a probe: train a small readout from the
hidden state to a physical variable, and report its accuracy. That evidence is weak, and its weakness
has a clean demonstration in our own setting. Six randomly initialised DreamerV3 models, which have
learned nothing at all, contain a direction correlating with the pendulum's energy at up to $0.908$.

The gap is that decodability says nothing about the model's dynamics. A quantity can be readable
from the hidden state while the model's own transition map destroys it every step. What we want to
know is whether the model's dynamics \emph{preserve} the quantity, and whether that preservation
does any work.

We look for a \textbf{flow-conserved quantity}: a scalar $C$ of the latent that stays constant under
the model's own one-step transition, found without ever showing the model a physical label. Three
properties make it checkable. It should be reproducible across seeds. It should vanish when the
physics does. And enforcing it should improve the model's predictions rather than damage them.

We test all three on a reference DreamerV3 \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3} trained on $64\times64$ video
of a gymnasium pendulum, with no actor, no critic, and no physical supervision. After training we
freeze the model and only read from it.

Our results are the following.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Recovery is reproducible, and the level is not the evidence.} At a pre-registered
extraction dimension, three unseen seeds recover a quantity correlating with true energy at $0.973$,
$0.967$, and $0.975$, with a within-trajectory drift of $10^{-4}$. Six untrained models span $0.170$
to $0.908$. The trained seeds span $0.008$ and the untrained draws span $0.738$, so training buys
consistency rather than the presence of the information.

\item \textbf{The method returns nothing when there is nothing to find.} On a matched dissipative
model, chosen by a damping rule we fixed before training anything, four statistics separate with no
overlap between seeds, including a $2400\times$ difference in drift.

\item \textbf{The recovered quantity is causally load-bearing, on two models of three.} Projecting
the latent onto its level set during imagination lowers rollout error by 2.9--3.5\% on every seed,
while the same projection with a random polynomial of matched complexity raises it by 53.9\% at the
median of sixty draws. Against each model's own twenty-draw null the recovered law is better than
every random law on two seeds and at the 15th percentile on the third, where 7 of 20 random laws
also improve the rollout. We report that split rather than the median.

\item \textbf{The correction acts on directions the latent does not make prominent, and it
reproduces functionally without reproducing coordinate-wise.} The projection pushes hardest where a
displacement most damages the model's own rollout ($+0.29$ to $+0.69$ across seeds), and at a 40-step
horizon those directions anti-correlate with variance ($-0.36$, $-0.26$, $-0.41$), an effect that
decays with horizon. Across seeds the basis-free quantities agree closely (recovery to $0.008$, edit
slope to $0.016$, gradient curvature to $0.046$) while a selector defined on ranked coordinates
changes sign. Identically trained models implement the same physical structure in different internal
charts.

\item \textbf{Two things do not carry over, and we report them.} The flow-generation condition that
motivates our selection rule turns out to buy reproducibility rather than accuracy: an ablation
holding everything else fixed shows conservation alone recovers the energy nearly as well and edits
just as effectively, but varies $4.4\times$ more across seeds. And a mechanism we established on a
much smaller recurrent model (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), which predicts trajectory error from the
accumulated invariant error weighted by the system's frequency sensitivity, does not separate from
its unweighted form at this sample size.
\end{enumerate}

We wrote every criterion above down before the run that tested it, including the ones that
failed. Section~\ref{sec:limits} reports a case where we refuted our own central claim on a bad
statistic and had to withdraw the refutation.
