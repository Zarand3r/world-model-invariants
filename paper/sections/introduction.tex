\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

World models learn to predict future observations and can use those predictions for planning. Good
video prediction, however, does not by itself show that a model has learned the underlying dynamics
of the system it observes. That distinction matters most when we ask the model to predict far beyond
the trajectories it saw during training.

A common way to look for physical structure is to fit a probe from the hidden state to a physical
variable. Probe accuracy tells us what information a latent representation retains, but not whether
the model's transition uses that information. Our own baseline makes this problem concrete. Six
randomly initialized DreamerV3 models contain polynomial functions of their latent state that
correlate with pendulum energy at up to $0.908$. A strong correlation can therefore appear even
before learning.

We ask a stronger question: does a trained model contain a scalar function of its latent state that
its own transition approximately preserves, and does preserving that scalar improve prediction? We
study DreamerV3 \citep{hafner2023dreamerv3} trained on $64\times64$ video from a Gymnasium pendulum,
with no actor, critic, or physical supervision. After training, we freeze the model and analyze its
latent dynamics.

We test the claim in three ways.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Recovery.} At an extraction dimension fixed before evaluating these models, three
independently trained conservative models recover scalars with energy correlations of $0.973$,
$0.967$, and $0.975$. The recovered scalar has within-trajectory variance equal to
$1.1\times10^{-4}$ of its total variance along observation-conditioned trajectories. Six untrained
models span $0.170$--$0.908$. Training therefore makes the recovered structure reproducible; it does
not simply make energy decodable.

\item \textbf{Dissipative control.} We apply the same search to models trained on a damped pendulum,
with the damping level fixed by a rule chosen before training. Within the same degree-4 polynomial
family, the search finds no approximately conserved candidate. Four statistics separate the
conservative and damped models without seed overlap.

\item \textbf{Intervention.} The recovered scalar drifts when the model imagines forward without new
observations. Projecting the latent state back onto the initial level set of that scalar lowers
rollout error by 2.9--3.5\% on all three conservative models. Twenty matched random polynomial
constraints usually increase error. The recovered constraint beats every random draw on two models;
on the third, the specificity result is weak.
\end{enumerate}

Two additional results narrow the interpretation. Basis-independent properties of the recovered
scalar reproduce across seeds more reliably than selectors tied to individual latent coordinates.
The flow-generation term in our extraction rule changes average recovery little but reduces
seed-to-seed variation. We also find that accumulated invariant error predicts rollout degradation,
but our data do not distinguish a frequency-weighted version of that mechanism from a simpler
unweighted one.
