\section{Ablations and limitations}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{The flow-generation term mainly reduces seed-to-seed variation.} Our default extraction
asks $C$ both to remain constant along trajectories and to satisfy $F \approx B\nabla C$ with
antisymmetric $B$. We isolate the second condition by holding the candidate family, data, dimension,
polynomial degree, and $\alpha$ grid fixed and selecting $C$ either by conservation alone or by the
joint criterion.

Conservation alone recovers energy at $0.950$, compared with $0.973$ for the joint rule, and gives a
slightly better average intervention slope ($-0.087$ versus $-0.077$, winning on two of three
models). The main difference is reproducibility: recovery varies by $0.035$ across seeds under
conservation alone and by $0.008$ under the joint rule. Conservation drives both recovery and
intervention performance; the flow-generation criterion contributes little to mean accuracy but
reduces seed-to-seed variation.

\paragraph{The pairing residual is a diagnostic, not a recovery score.} As the extraction dimension
increases from 6 to 12, energy recovery improves from $0.721$ to $0.967$ while the pairing residual
worsens from $0.738$ to $0.876$. Recovery already reaches $0.967$ at dimension 8, where the residual
is lower at $0.813$.

The extra dimensions do not explain this pattern by carrying energy while resisting the Hamiltonian
fit. Directions 7--12 account for 44.9\% of the residual and 43.5\% of the flow, nearly the same
share. The pairing residual therefore does not track recovery quality across extraction dimension, so
we use it only as a diagnostic. Appendix~\ref{sec:repro} reports the full sweeps.

\paragraph{Energy information can lie outside the highest-variance directions.} On two of the three
models, principal directions 7--12 carry about twice as much energy information as the top six
directions: $2.23$ times and $2.13$ times as much. On the third model, the ratio reverses to $0.91$.
We report the three models separately because a median would hide that sign change. This result is
consistent with the causal analysis in Section~\ref{sec:leverage}, but it is not uniform across
seeds.

\paragraph{The frequency-weighted mechanism remains unresolved.} Accumulated invariant error predicts
which rollouts degrade most. An integral predictor reaches $R^2 = 0.187$, compared with $0.021$ for a
fitted power law in time at the horizon where both perform best, and shuffling destroys the relation.

The current sample cannot tell whether the relevant integral needs the frequency weighting found in
the earlier recurrent model or whether an unweighted integral is enough. In a nested comparison with
400 bootstrap resamples, the weighted term adds explanatory power beyond the unweighted term in 1 of
9 seed--horizon combinations; the unweighted term adds beyond the weighted term in 0 of 9. At
$n = 512$, we therefore treat the mechanism as unresolved. Appendix~\ref{sec:gru} describes the
stronger result from the earlier recurrent model.

\paragraph{Scope.} This study covers one physical system, one degree of freedom, one architecture,
and a much shorter training schedule than standard DreamerV3. The pendulum trajectories cross the
separatrix: 17\% rotate rather than librate, so we restrict the mechanism analysis to the librating
trajectories. We have not tested contacts, multiple interacting objects, nonzero actions, or systems
whose frequency varies enough to identify the frequency-weighting mechanism.
Sections~\ref{sec:leverage} and~\ref{sec:charts} use only the conservative models.
