\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Probing.} Linear and nonlinear probes can decode physical or linguistic variables from
hidden states \citep{alain2017probing}, but probe accuracy mixes information stored by the
representation with the capacity of the probe itself. Control tasks \citep{hewitt2019control} and
later critiques \citep{belinkov2022probing} make this limitation explicit. Our untrained DreamerV3
baseline plays the same role for invariant extraction: four of six randomly initialized models exceed
the success threshold we had previously used. Work on emergent world representations in sequence
models \citep{li2023othello,nanda2023othello} goes beyond decoding by intervening on learned
representations. Our rollout intervention in Section~\ref{sec:intervention} follows that logic.

\paragraph{Equation discovery and structured dynamics models.} Equation-discovery methods such as
SINDy \citep{brunton2016sindy} infer governing equations directly from observed trajectories.
Hamiltonian and Lagrangian neural networks \citep{greydanus2019hnn,cranmer2020lnn} instead build
physical structure into the architecture so that conservation holds by construction. These approaches
model the physical system itself. We treat an already trained world model as the object of study and
ask what its learned transition approximately preserves. Our extraction also uses standard
linear-algebraic ideas related to Koopman and dynamic mode methods \citep{schmid2010dmd}.

\paragraph{Evaluation of world models.} Dreamer-style world models
\citep{hafner2023dreamerv3,ha2018world} and planning-based learned models
\citep{schrittwieser2020muzero} are usually evaluated by prediction quality or downstream return.
Those metrics do not show whether the latent transition preserves physical invariants. The
disentanglement literature \citep{locatello2019challenging} similarly shows how apparent structure in
a representation can survive weak controls. Our damped models directly test whether the
invariant-search procedure still returns a conserved-looking scalar when the training dynamics have
no nontrivial first integral of the kind we seek.

The dynamical-systems tools we use are standard. The main result is empirical: a pixel-trained
DreamerV3 develops a reproducible energy-like invariant with measurable dynamical relevance, and
matched controls rule out simple decodability as the explanation.
