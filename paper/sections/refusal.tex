\section{The method returns nothing when the physics is absent}
\label{sec:refusal}

A search that always returns a conserved quantity would explain the previous section without any physics. We
test that directly by running the identical pipeline on a world model trained on a
\emph{dissipative} pendulum, where no non-trivial conserved function of the state exists: every
trajectory spirals into the same fixed point, so any continuous function constant along trajectories
is constant on the whole basin.

\paragraph{Choosing the damping without looking at the outcome.} Strong damping collapses the
trajectories into a small neighbourhood of the fixed point, which makes the latent rank-deficient
and manufactures directions that are trivially constant. A refusal produced that way would say
nothing about the method. We therefore fixed a rule before training anything: take the largest
damping ratio whose late-window spread across trajectories stays above 25\% of the conservative
reference, measured on simulator states alone with no model involved. The natural choice
$\zeta = 0.15$ fails that rule outright, leaving 0\% of the reference spread. The rule selects
$\zeta = 0.03$, which removes 17\% of the energy per period and leaves 34\% of the spread. All three
dissipative models pass the same training acceptance checks as the conservative ones.

\paragraph{Four statistics separate with no overlap between seeds.} Table~\ref{tab:refusal} reports
them. The recovered $C$ drifts $2400\times$ more on the dissipative models, and its held-out
invariance ratio is $0.987$, meaning the model conserves nothing. That is the right answer.

\paragraph{Takeaway.} The pipeline discriminates conservative from dissipative dynamics under
matched architecture, training budget, observation modality, and extraction protocol. The
correlation in Section~\ref{sec:recovery} is therefore not an artefact of a search that always
returns something.
