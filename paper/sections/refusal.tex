\section{The same search fails under matched damping}
\label{sec:refusal}

A search that produces a conserved-looking scalar for almost any latent representation could explain
Section~\ref{sec:recovery} without telling us anything about the learned dynamics. We test that
possibility with matched models trained on a damped pendulum.

For the damped system, every trajectory converges to the same fixed point. Any continuous scalar
that stays constant along all trajectories in the basin must therefore be constant throughout that
basin, so the state has no nontrivial first integral for our search to recover.

\paragraph{Choosing the damping level.} Very strong damping creates a separate problem: trajectories
collapse into a small neighborhood of the fixed point, making the latent representation
rank-deficient and creating directions that look constant for trivial reasons. We therefore fixed
the damping level using only simulator states, before training any damped model. We chose the
largest damping ratio whose late-window spread across trajectories remained above 25\% of the
conservative reference.

A stronger value, $\zeta=0.15$, leaves 0\% of that spread and fails this criterion. The rule selects
$\zeta=0.03$, which removes 17\% of the energy per period while retaining 34\% of the conservative
spread. All three damped models pass the same training checks as the conservative models.

\paragraph{The conservative and damped models separate on all four statistics.}
Table~\ref{tab:refusal} shows the result. The recovered $C$ varies about $2400\times$ more within
damped trajectories than within conservative ones. The damped models also have a held-out invariance
ratio of $0.983$--$0.994$, compared with $0.0027$--$0.0121$ for the conservative models. Within the
degree-4 family we search, the damped models contain no approximately conserved candidate.

The two arms use the same architecture, training budget, observation modality, and extraction
procedure. The difference is the dynamics in the training data. This control does not prove that a
damped model contains no conserved quantity of any possible form; it shows that our pipeline does
not return an approximately conserved degree-4 scalar when the underlying dynamics remove the
corresponding conservation law.
