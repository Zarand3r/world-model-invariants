# E19 preregistration -- does the *shadow* Hamiltonian explain the supervised probe's failure?

Written **before** any E19 number exists. Registered 2026-08-27.

## The question

E18 established the paper's sharpest result: a ridge probe fitted to true pendulum energy reaches
`rho_E` = 0.9999 -- an essentially perfect readout -- yet the model's own transition preserves it
**6.7x worse** than a label-free scalar (`rho_obs` 4.58e-2 against 6.85e-3), and acting on it never
repairs the rollout. E18 reports the dissociation. It does not explain it.

**Hypothesis (H_shadow).** The training data is generated by gymnasium's semi-implicit (symplectic)
Euler update. A symplectic integrator does not conserve the textbook Hamiltonian `H`; it exactly
conserves a nearby *shadow* Hamiltonian `H~ = H + O(dt)`. If the world model has learned the
integrator's map, then the quantity its transition preserves is `H~`, not `H`. On that reading the
supervised probe is not merely a worse invariant -- it is fitting **the wrong target with perfect
precision**, and the label-free search wins because it optimises conservation and therefore lands
near `H~`.

This is not a new idea in this project: `scripts/make_pendulum_pixels.py` has carried the note since
the data was generated, alongside the measured ~8-12% oscillation of textbook `E` with no secular
drift. It has never been tested.

## The derivation, and how its sign is fixed

Free pendulum, `I = ml^2/3`, `V(q) = mg(l/2) cos q`, `p = I*thetadot`. The update is

    thetadot' = thetadot + (3g/2l) sin(theta) dt          (kick, uses theta_n)
    theta'    = theta + thetadot' dt                      (drift, uses thetadot_{n+1})

i.e. Lie-Trotter splitting `phi = phi_T o phi_V`. To first order the modified Hamiltonian is
`H~ = H + (dt/2){T,V} + O(dt^2)` with `{T,V} = -(p/I) V'(q)`, giving a correction proportional to

    thetadot * sin(theta),   with magnitude (dt/2) * mg(l/2) = (0.05/2) * 5 = **0.125**

**The sign is fixed empirically, not asserted.** We evaluate both signs on ground-truth trajectories
and keep whichever is better conserved. This is a check on the derivation, not a fitted parameter --
there is one binary choice and it is decided before any model is touched.

## Design: a coefficient sweep, so the control is built in

The obvious two-point test (probe fitted to `E` vs probe fitted to `H~`) has an obvious confound: a
probe fitted to `H~` might do better simply because `H~` is a *different function*, possibly easier
to represent in the degree-4 basis, with nothing to do with shadow physics.

So we do not run a two-point test. We sweep the target family

    T_c(theta, thetadot) = E(theta, thetadot) + c * thetadot * sin(theta)

over `c` in {-0.5, -0.25, -0.125, -0.0625, 0, +0.0625, +0.125, +0.25, +0.5}, which contains the
predicted shadow coefficient (|c| = 0.125), **both signs**, and wrong magnitudes at 2x and 4x either
way. `c = 0` is exactly E18's supervised probe. Everything else -- latent, PCA frame, degree-4 basis,
ridge, direction-matched repair, eps grid, warmup, analysis slice -- is held identical to E18.

The prediction is then about the **shape of a curve**, not about one number being smaller than
another.

## Registered predictions

**P1 (physics validation; no model involved).** On ground-truth states, the invariance ratio of
`T_c` -- `mean_traj[var_t T_c] / var_total[T_c]`, the statistic already used as `ratio_empirical`
elsewhere in this project -- is minimised at `|c| = 0.125` with one specific sign, and is at least
**2x** lower there than at `c = 0`.

> *If P1 fails, STOP.* The derivation is wrong and nothing downstream is interpretable. Record as a
> negative result and leave the mechanism unexplained.

**P2 (the key test).** `rho_obs` of the probe fitted to `T_c` is minimised **near the shadow
coefficient**, not at `c = 0` and not monotonically in `|c|`. Quantitatively: `argmin_c rho_obs` lies
at the shadow `c*` or an adjacent grid point, on **at least 2 of 3** model seeds.

**P3 (magnitude).** At `c = c*`, `rho_obs` is at least **2x** below its value at `c = 0` (4.58e-2),
on at least 2 of 3 seeds.

**P4 (registered, but not required for a positive verdict).** The repair effect on rollout energy
drift at `c = c*` is more negative than at `c = 0` (+26.8% median) on at least 2 of 3 seeds.

## Falsifiers, stated plainly

- **P1 fails** -> derivation wrong; abort; mechanism unexplained.
- **P2 fails** (the `rho_obs` minimum is at `c = 0`, or monotone in `|c|`, or scattered across seeds
  with no consistent location) -> **H_shadow is falsified.** The supervised probe's disadvantage is
  not explained by the integrator's modified Hamiltonian. E18's dissociation stands as an
  unexplained phenomenon and the paper says so.
- **P2 holds but P3 fails** -> the shadow correction points the right way but explains only a small
  part of the gap. Report as partial support with the residual quantified.

A monotone decrease of `rho_obs` with `|c|` regardless of sign is the specific artifact this design
exists to detect, and it counts as a falsification, not a success.

## Registered interpretation of a positive result -- stated now, so it cannot inflate later

Even full support for P1-P3 would establish something **narrower** than "the dissociation is
explained":

1. It shows that textbook `E` is the wrong conservation target for a symplectically-generated
   dataset, and that a probe inherits that error no matter how well it fits.
2. It does **not** show the model conserves `H~` exactly. The model is a learned approximation of
   the integrator, so its own invariant is its own modified quantity, of which the data
   integrator's `H~` is at best a first-order proxy. Any residual gap between `rho_obs` at `c*` and
   the label-free 6.85e-3 measures exactly that, and must be reported rather than glossed.
3. The label-free method's advantage would then have a name: it optimises conservation by the
   *learned* transition, so it is not restricted to targets a human knows how to write down.

The paper claim licensed by a positive result is therefore **"part of the supervised probe's
disadvantage is that it is fitted to the wrong invariant, and the size of the unexplained remainder
is R"** -- with R reported -- and not "the dissociation is explained".
