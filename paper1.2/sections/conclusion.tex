\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A world model trained only on pendulum video learns a conserved quantity, and it is the one its
\emph{simulator} conserves rather than the one physics prescribes. Vary the simulator's timestep and
the recovered coefficient follows the integrator's parameter-free prediction, slope
$2.484 \pm 0.058$ against $2.500$. What the model has internalised is the numerical scheme, not the
physics that scheme approximates.

That reframes a failure of interpretability rather than excusing it. A probe fitted to true energy at
$|\rho| = 0.9999$ identifies a direction the dynamics do not use, and enforcing it degrades the
model's imagined physics --- not because the probe is poor, but because it is fitted to a quantity
the generating process does not conserve. No probe hygiene detects that. Decodability and dynamical
structure are different properties, they come apart in every setting we could construct, and only a
measurement on the transition operator tells them apart.

The practical consequence is narrow. That a physical variable is decodable says little about whether
the model's rollouts respect it, and says least where it matters most: out of distribution, in
architectures that predict well without conserving, and when the variable comes from supervision
rather than from the dynamics. The operator statistic costs one autonomous step per state; it does
not, on this evidence, buy a better controller.
