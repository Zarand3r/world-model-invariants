\begin{abstract}
A world model can encode a quantity that its own dynamics never preserve. We test whether the energy
a probe decodes from a frozen DreamerV3 trained only on pendulum video is the energy its transition
preserves. A polynomial probe fitted to true energy reaches correlation $0.9999$, while the model's
transition preserves that direction $6.2\times$ worse than a scalar recovered without labels and
frozen before scoring. During autonomous rollouts, the recovered scalar drifts. Projecting the latent
back toward its level set cuts decoded physical energy drift by $55$ to $76\%$, whereas 0 of 60
matched random directions beat it. The recovery and the repair both transfer to a
two-degree-of-freedom oscillator. These results distinguish a scalar the dynamics use from one they
merely carry and expose a concrete failure mode of interpretability: a probe can certify
near-perfect recovery of a quantity the model's transition does not preserve.
\end{abstract}
